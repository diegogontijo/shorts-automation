## What if you were raised by bears your entire life?

Day one, a grizzly finds you in the forest. She sniffs you, puffs hot breath in your face. She lies down, pulls you into her fur. You survive the night.

Year one, you're riding on her back, gripping fur. She weaves through rivers with you clinging on. Her two cubs wrestle you daily. You learn to fight back or get hurt. Your skin toughens up fast.

Year three, your first real hunt. Salmon run. You wade freezing into water. Swipe with your hands like claws. Your human brain gives you strategy. You catch three before the cubs catch one.

Year six, your fingernails have hardened into claws, thick and sharp from constant climbing. Trees are your escape route. Your arms are unnaturally powerful.

Year 18, you're massive now. Hair wild, covered in scars and dirt. You mark trees with scratches and scent. Your territory. Other bears recognize your marks and stay away.

Year 20. Tourists spot you from a distance, filming with trembling hands. One roar, one charge, they're gone, screaming through the trees. Within hours, their shaky footage goes viral.

## What actually happens to your body when you bite your nails?

Number one, when you bite, those germs go straight to your stomach. Stomach flu vibes, cramps, and some pretty nasty bacteria that you definitely don't want.

Number two, you're creating tiny wounds around your fingers. These become gateways for infections that cause irritation and inflammation. You're turning your fingertips into a playground for bacteria.

Number three, your teeth are paying the price. Cracked enamel, misaligned bite, and jaw problems that'll cost you thousands at the dentist.

Number four, your body interprets it as a sign of stress or anxiety. It's not just a habit. It's a way for your mind to express constant tension.

And here's the creepy part. Most people bite their nails without even realizing it. Your body is literally eating itself from stress.
